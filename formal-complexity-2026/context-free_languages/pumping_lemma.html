The pumping lemma for context-free languages

The pumping lemma for context-free languages is stated as follows.

  1. If \(L ⊆ Σ^{*}\) is a context-free language, then there is a pumping length \(p\) (which must be a positive integer: \(p ≥ 1\)), such that for any \(s ∈ L\), if \(|s| ≥ p\), then \(s\) can be written \[s = u · v · w · x · y\] for strings \(u, v, w, x, y ∈ Σ^{*}\) of which the following properties all hold:

    • \(|v| > 0\) or \(|x| > 0\) (i.e., at least one of \(v\) or \(x\) is not the empty string)
    • \(|v · w · x| ≤ p\)
    • \(u · v^{n} · w · x^{n} · y ∈ L\) for every \(n ≥ 0\)

Again, \(|s|\) is the length of \(s\). Thus the pumping lemma for context-free languages says that once the length of a string \(s\) in the context-free language \(L\) reaches \(L\)’s pumping length \(p\), \(s\) can be broken down into parts \(u, v, w, x, y\), such that either \(v\) or \(x\) (or both) is of length at least 1, and \(v\) and \(x\) can be simultaneously pumped: they can either both be deleted or both copied any number of times (as long as the number of times is the same for both), and the resulting string will still be in \(L\).

An example

Let’s define the following language, \(L_{anbncn}\): \[L_{anbncn} = \{a^{n}b^{n}c^{n} ∣ n ≥ 0\}\] \(L_{anbncn}\) has as elements strings such as \(ϵ\), \(abc\), \(aabbcc\), \(aaabbbccc\), etc. It does not have as elements strings such as \(a\), \(b\), \(aabb\), \(aabcc\), \(aabbccc\), etc. (or strings in which the \(a\)’s don’t come before the \(b\)’s or the \(b\)’s don’t come before the \(c\)’s). To be in \(L_{anbncn}\), your number of \(a\)’s has to be the same as your number of \(b\)’s, which both have to be the same as your number of \(c\)’s; and your \(a\)’s must come before your \(b\)’s, which must come before your \(c\)’s.

\(L_{anbncn}\) is not context-free, which we can show using the above pumping lemma.

First we assume (for contradiction) that \(L_{anbncn}\) is context-free. Then there would be a pumping length \(p\), such that for any \(s ∈ L_{anbncn}\) where \(|s| ≥ p\), \(s = u · v · w · x · y\) for some \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(x\), and \(y\), where \(|v · w · x| ≤ p\) (and \(v ≠ ϵ\) or \(x ≠ ϵ\)), and such that \(u · v^{n} · w · x^{n} · y ∈ L_{anbncn}\) (for any \(n ≥ 0\)). Let’s assume we have the pumping length \(p\). Now consider the string: \[a^{p}b^{p}c^{p}\] This string is in \(L_{anbncn}\), by definition (since we have \(a^{n}b^{n}c^{n} ∈ L_{anbncn}\) for every \(n ≥ 0\), and \(p\) is some \(n\)). The length of this string is greater than \(p\) (in fact, it is \(3p\)); hence, the pumping lemma applies. Therefore, there are substrings \(u\), \(v\), \(w\), \(x\), and \(y\), such that:

  • \(|v · w · x| ≤ p\)
  • \(v ≠ ϵ\) or \(x ≠ ϵ\)
  • \(a^{p}b^{p}c^{p} = u · v · w · x · y\)
  • \(u · v^{n} · w · x^{n} · y ∈ L_{anbncn}\) for every \(n ≥ 0\)

Because \(|v · w · x| ≤ p\), one of five things must be the case:

  • \(v · w · x\) consists only of \(a\)’s
  • \(v · w · x\) consists only of \(b\)’s
  • \(v · w · x\) consists only of \(c\)’s
  • \(v · w · x\) consists of some \(a\)’s followed by some \(b\)’s
  • \(v · w · x\) consists of some \(b\)’s followed by some \(c\)’s

Crucially, the substring \(v · w · x\) can not consist of some \(a\)’s followed by some \(b\)’s followed by some \(c\)’s; it simply isn’t long enough to straddle all three sections of \(s\)! Why? Because its length is at most \(p\), but each section of \(s\)—i.e., the \(a\) section, the \(b\) section, and the \(c\) section—is \(p\) symbols long.

As a result, pumping \(v\) and \(x\) (i.e., by choosing \(n = 0\) or \(n > 1\)) will do one of the following to \(s\):

  • delete or add \(a\)’s
  • delete or add \(b\)’s
  • delete or add \(c\)’s
  • delete or add \(a\)’s as well as \(b\)’s
  • delete or add \(b\)’s as well as \(c\)’s

But it won’t result in doing the same thing to all three symbols \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\). Thus the resulting string won’t be in \(L_{anbncn}\), contradicting our assumption. \(L_{anbncn}\) can’t be context-free after all.

The pumping lemma for context-free languages is stated as follows.

(@ex-pumping-lemma) If $L ⊆ Σ^{*}$ is a context-free language, then there is a *pumping length* $p$ (which must be a positive integer: $p ≥ 1$), such that for any $s ∈ L$, if $|s| ≥ p$, then $s$ can be written
    $$s = u · v · w · x · y$$
    for strings $u, v, w, x, y ∈ Σ^{*}$ of which the following properties all hold:

    - $|v| > 0$ or $|x| > 0$ (i.e., at least one of $v$ or $x$ is not the empty string)
    - $|v · w · x| ≤ p$
    - $u · v^{n} · w · x^{n} · y ∈ L$ for every $n ≥ 0$

Again, $|s|$ is the *length* of $s$.
Thus the pumping lemma for context-free languages says that once the length of a string $s$ in the context-free language $L$ reaches $L$'s pumping length $p$, $s$ can be broken down into parts $u, v, w, x, y$, such that either $v$ or $x$ (or both) is of length at least 1, and $v$ and $x$ can be simultaneously *pumped*:
they can either both be deleted or both copied any number of times (as long as the number of times is the same for both), and the resulting string will still be in $L$.
